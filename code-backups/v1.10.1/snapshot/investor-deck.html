Pricing

Four prices, one of which is zero

This is the price list running in production today, not a target. Every rate below is the rate in backend/src/services/fee.service.js.

Who pays What for Rate

Patient Three equal monthly instalments, no fee of any kind R0
Patient, if late Late fee on the overdue amount, compounding monthly 5% / month
Blended take on facilitated volume 6.2%

Blended take assumes 60% of volume is funded through the marketplace and therefore earns both fees. Late fees are modelled at zero revenue: they exist to price the cost of a broken schedule, not to fund the business.

No subscription. No setup fee. No monthly minimum.

A practice pays only when a patient who would otherwise have walked out proceeds. That removes the single largest objection in the sales conversation and is why a 15 minute demo converts at all. It also means a practice can never be underwater on PaySick, which keeps churn structural rather than economic.

What changed, and why

Earlier decks quoted an arrangement fee of two to four percent against a 10% blended take. The platform charges 5% to the provider and nothing to the patient, which puts the real blended take at 6.2%. That number is lower and it is the one the code produces.

The funnel is published, not asserted

55% of sourced practices yield a public business email. 12% reply across a four-touch sequence. 40% of replies take a demo, 30% of demos sign, 80% of signings activate inside 60 days. That is one activated provider per 158 leads sourced.

The constraint is review minutes, not spend

Sourcing, enrichment and drafting cost R1.30 per lead. Every message is human gated through the approve queue at roughly 90 seconds each. At 120 leads a day that is 250 touches, 6.3 hours, one dedicated reviewer. That single hire is what moves CAC from R4,800 to R2,700.

The gap between the configured caps and the funded plan is R201.6M of annual facilitated volume and R12.5M of gross revenue at exit run-rate. The caps are two integers in a config file.

Defensibility

Why Big BNPL Can't Just Walk In

Four structural moats protect PaySick from retail BNPL players entering healthcare.

Regulated Complexity

Healthcare payment facilitation requires POPIA (medical data sensitivity), HPCSA provider verification, CPA compliance, and medical aid coordination. Retail BNPL needs 12-18 months of regulatory retooling.

Provider as Distribution

PaySick is B2B2C. The doctor refers the patient at point of care. Consumer brand awareness is irrelevant: provider relationships are the channel with high switching costs.

Medical ≠ Retail Risk

Assessing a R45K surgery arrangement with clinical urgency and medical aid coordination is fundamentally different from a R2K retail purchase. Generic models face massive adverse selection.

Marketplace Neutrality

As marketplace infrastructure, bigger BNPL/lending players aren't threats, they're potential funding partners ON the platform. PaySick wins regardless of who funds arrangements.

Proprietary AI Risk IP

Shield™: the risk engine behind every arrangement

Shield is PaySick's proprietary risk architecture, purpose-built for healthcare payment facilitation. It is our primary IP moat: it cannot be replicated without years of healthcare-specific operational data, and it runs in production today.

Five gates on every arrangement

  • Provider Gate: tier and billing-agreement checks, plus concentration limits capping any single provider at 5% of the book and any procedure type at 20%
  • Affordability Gate: verified income no older than 90 days, assessed against the patient's obligations before any amount is confirmed
  • Urgency Gate: clinical urgency is scored so medically necessary care is treated differently from elective procedures
  • Tariff Gate: the facilitatable ceiling is computed from scheme tariffs, preventing over-billing at source rather than detecting it later
  • Circuit Breaker Gate: disbursements halt automatically on anomaly detection, with a human review queue for escalated edge cases

Tariff billing controls

Shipped controls that sit around the five gates:

  • Designated Service Provider status validated in real time
  • Provider billing agreement required before any payout
  • Patient-facing gap disclosure acknowledged before commitment
  • Explanation of Benefits reconciled after the procedure
  • Full audit trail on every control decision

Two-stage payout architecture

Precision disbursement, not a single lump transfer:

  • Provisional payout of 80% on procedure confirmation
  • Final 20% released once the claim reconciles
  • Funding partners on the marketplace inherit these controls
  • Every decision is replayable for audit and dispute

"Shield's disbursement controls create an arrangement sustainability advantage no generic platform can replicate, without years of healthcare-specific operational data."
